Local note

What Fairfield's Tap Water Does to Your Fixtures

Fairfield treats water from Lake Berryessa and the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta at the Waterman and North Bay Regional plants. The City's 2022 report lists average hardness at 162 ppm, with a range of 72 to 229 ppm, which falls in the hard band on the common USGS scale.

For a home, hard water means scale on heating elements, crusty aerators and shorter water heater life. If you see white buildup or your tank rumbles, ask about water heater repair or a water softener and filtration setup.

Fairfield emergency prep

Before We Arrive in Fairfield

These steps limit damage while you wait for a plumber. Do only what is safe.

1

Find the main shutoff

It is usually where the water line enters the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

2

Cut power near water

If water reaches outlets or the water heater's electrical connection, stay out and switch off the breaker only if the panel is dry.

3

Smell gas? Leave

Get everyone outside and call 911. Do not use switches or phones inside.

4

Move valuables up

Lift rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor and open cabinet doors under the sink.

5

Take quick photos

Pictures of the damage help if you later talk with your insurer.

For a sewer backup, stop using water in the house. Fairfield residents can also call the City's water and sewer line at 707-428-7346 to ask who handles the main in the street.

Sewer Backups: Who Owns What in Fairfield

Fairfield sewer service involves the Fairfield-Suisun Sewer District and City Public Works. The district's treatment plant sits on Chadbourne Road in Fairfield, and it does not maintain private sewer laterals.

That matters because the lateral from your building to the main belongs to the property owner. If a backup is in your own line, you hire a plumber. If it is in the public main, the City or district handles it, and a camera check can show which side the blockage is on. Sewer line repair is the next step when the lateral itself is damaged.
