Start With the Water Meter: A Quick Test Anyone Can Run

Before anyone brings equipment, the meter can tell you whether water is leaking at all. The test takes a few minutes and costs nothing but patience.

1

Stop every use of water

Close all taps and make sure the dishwasher, washing machine and ice maker are idle. Nobody should flush a toilet during the test.

2

Read the meter

Lift the lid of the meter box near the curb. Look for the small dial or triangle that spins when even a thin stream passes through.

3

Wait and look again

Give it about twenty minutes without using water. If the dial has moved, or the triangle is still turning, water is going somewhere it should not.

4

Close the house valve and recheck

If the meter stops, the leak is on the house side of the valve. If it keeps turning, the leak is in the buried line between the meter and the house.

Signs of a Leak You Cannot See

Most hidden leaks announce themselves long before a ceiling comes down. These are the clues callers describe most often.

A bill that keeps creeping up

Usage that climbs month after month with no change in habits points to water escaping somewhere. Compare the last several bills, not just the latest.

The sound of running water

A faint hiss or trickle at night, when the house is quiet and every fixture is off, usually means pressurized water is leaking inside a wall or under the floor.

Warm or damp spots

A floor patch that feels warm under bare feet can mean a leaking hot line. Damp carpet edges and baseboards that swell tell a similar story.

Stains, odors and peeling paint

A musty smell, a brown ceiling ring or paint that bubbles on a wall all suggest moisture that has been present for a while.

How a Leak Gets Found Without Opening Walls

Good detection is about narrowing the search before any drywall or concrete is touched. Several methods are combined, because no single tool answers every question.

Listening for the leak

Acoustic sensors pick up the sound water makes as it escapes a pressurized pipe. A technician moves along floors, walls and the yard, and the sound grows louder as the sensor nears the source.

Pressure testing the lines

Isolating one section of pipe and watching whether it holds pressure shows which branch is losing water. This separates a hot line problem from a cold line problem in a few steps.

Thermal imaging and moisture readings

A thermal camera shows temperature differences behind a surface, which is useful for hot water leaks. A moisture meter confirms whether a wet-looking area is actually wet or just stained from an old event.

Camera work and tracer gas

A small camera can inspect drain lines that may be the real source of a damp area. For stubborn cases, a harmless gas can be introduced into an isolated line so a sniffer can follow it to the exact point.

Water Escaping Now? Do This Before We Arrive

A few quick moves limit damage while a plumber is on the way. None of them need tools.

1

Close the main shutoff

Turn the house valve clockwise until it stops. If it is stuck or leaking, leave it and tell the dispatcher.

2

Protect the water heater

If the leak seems to be on the hot side, switch the heater's power off or set a gas unit to pilot so the tank is not heating an empty system.

3

Clear the floor

Lift rugs, move boxes and pull furniture off wet areas. Wet cardboard and carpet padding hold moisture and start to smell quickly.

4

Photograph the meter and the damage

A quick photo of the meter dial and any stains helps the technician and gives you a record for your insurer.

5

Stay clear of wet electrical

If water is near outlets, a panel or appliances, do not touch them. Tell the dispatcher so the visit can be planned around it.

If a ceiling is bulging with water, poke a small hole at its lowest point over a bucket. A controlled drip does less harm than a collapse.

What Hard Water Does Inside Suisun City Pipes

The City's 2025 water quality report shows tap water at 170 ppm hardness, which is about 9.9 grains per gallon. On the common USGS scale that sits in the hard band, close to the top of it.

Minerals leave crust on faucet seats, supply connectors and heater fittings, and a crusted joint can weep slowly for months before anyone sees a puddle. When a technician finds white scale around a drip, it is a clue worth following. A water softener and filtration system slows that buildup on new connections.

Source: City of Suisun City 2025 Water Quality Report, hardness sampled March 18, 2025.

Soft Ground, Older Pipe and Why Leaks Start Where They Do

Suisun City was built on marshy land beside Suisun Marsh. Local history records the railroad roadbed across that marsh settling 6 to 12 inches a year in the late 1800s before fill and drainage steadied it. Ground that shifts, even slowly, puts stress on buried pipe joints, so a line that runs through soft soil deserves a closer look when pressure drops.

The city also grew about eightfold between 1970 and 1990, so much of the housing stock dates from the late 1970s through the 1990s. Supply pipe from those decades is now old enough that pinhole leaks and failing joints are a fair suspect. If you see a pattern of leaks in one house, a repiping consultation may be a better plan than patching one spot at a time.

What You Can Check Yourself and What Needs Equipment

Homeowners can rule a lot in or out. Locating a leak behind a finished surface is where tools earn their keep.

Worth trying on your own

  • Run the meter test and note the result
  • Add food coloring to the toilet tank and wait for color in the bowl
  • Check under sinks with a dry paper towel
  • Look at the water heater base and connections
  • Compare the last several bills

Better left to a technician

  • Pinpointing a leak inside a wall or ceiling
  • Locating a leak under a slab or driveway
  • Tracing a buried line across the yard
  • Testing pressure on isolated pipe sections
  • Writing up findings your insurer may ask for

Opening drywall on a guess often means two holes instead of one. Finding the exact point first keeps the repair small.

After the Leak Is Found: Your Repair Options

A confirmed location lets you choose a repair that fits the problem. A short split in a wall line is a small fix, while a failed line under the floor is a different job. The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), sets which pipe materials and fittings may replace a failed section, so the repair has to match it.
