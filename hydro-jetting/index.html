When a Cable Keeps Failing, the Pipe Wall Is the Problem

A cable punches a hole through a clog, but it leaves most of the coating on the pipe wall. Grease, soap and mineral scale stay behind, and the opening narrows again within weeks. That is why the same drain can feel fixed on Monday and slow by the end of the month.

Hydro jetting attacks the coating itself. A hose with a nozzle sends pressurized water in all directions inside the pipe, so the walls are scoured and the debris is flushed down and out. The result is a pipe closer to its full diameter, not just a thin channel through the middle.

Line Backing Up Before the Jet Truck Arrives

A few quick moves keep damage small until the crew is on site.

1

Stop sending water down

Hold off on showers, dishwashers and laundry. A full line has no room for more.

2

Find the cleanout if you can

It is usually a capped pipe near the house or by the property line. Do not open it if the line is full, because the pressure can push sewage out.

3

Keep people and pets back

Dirty water from a backed-up line carries bacteria. Set off the area and keep it dry.

4

Tell the dispatcher what was tried

Mention past cable visits, chemical cleaners and how long the problem has lasted. It helps the plumber plan the job.

What Happens During a Jetting Visit

The process is quick to explain, even if the equipment is large.

1

Access the line

The nozzle goes in through a cleanout or a removed fixture. A clear path makes the work faster and safer.

2

Check with a camera if needed

When the pipe's condition is unknown, a short camera run first confirms it can take the pressure.

3

Work the nozzle through

Forward jets cut through the blockage while rear jets pull the hose along and wash debris toward the exit.

4

Flush and confirm

Water runs through the line afterward to prove it drains freely, and you hear what the plumber saw.

Pipes That Should Not Be Jetted Without a Look First

High pressure is a strong tool, and old or damaged pipe can fail under it. Brittle clay, cast iron flaking from the inside and sections that have shifted or pulled apart may break open when jetted blind.

What a camera check rules out

A short run with a sewer camera shows breaks, belled joints and heavy root intrusion. If the pipe is sound, jetting goes ahead with confidence. If it is damaged, the plan shifts to sewer line repair so you do not pay to clean a pipe that is failing.

Why this matters on soft ground

Suisun City sits next to Suisun Marsh, and local history records the old marshy ground settling for decades before fill and drainage stabilized it. Ground movement can stress buried pipe over time, so lines under settled areas are worth looking at before they are jetted hard. The California Plumbing Code, drawn from the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), expects drain lines to have cleanout access, which also makes jetting safer and simpler.

Local note

Local Water Leaves Scale Inside Drain Lines

The City's 2025 water quality report lists tap water hardness at 170 ppm, about 9.9 grains per gallon, which sits in the hard band of the USGS scale. Hard water deposits mineral film inside pipe, and that film catches soap and grease. For lines that clog on a regular schedule, jetting is one way to remove buildup that a cable cannot reach.

A softener upstream reduces new scale, but it does not clean what is already there. The two steps work together, and the water softener page covers the options.

What Affects the Price of Hydro Jetting

Jetting is a specialized job with equipment costs, so the price depends on a few factors.

Length and size of the line. A longer or wider pipe takes more time and water.
Access point. A cleanout is simple. Pulling a toilet to reach the line adds work.
Buildup level. Light grease takes fewer passes than packed scale or dense roots.
Camera work. A pre-jet or post-jet video check adds time and may add to the price.
