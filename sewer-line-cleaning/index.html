Every Drain Gurgling Is a Main Line Warning

The main line collects everything the house sends out. When it clogs, the lowest opening shows it first.

Floor drain or tub fills with dirty water

The lowest fixture sits closest to the blockage. Water finds the nearest way up and out.

Toilets gurgle and sinks bubble

Trapped air is being forced backward through the traps because the line downstream cannot take it.

Flushing sends water into the shower

A flush that shows up in the tub means the main is not moving it away.

Several drains slow at once

One slow sink points to a local clog. Three slow fixtures together point to the shared line.

Main Line Backed Up? Do This While You Wait

The goal is to keep more water out of the line and keep the mess contained.

1

Stop every water source

Hold the laundry, shower and dishwasher. Even a rinse adds to the overflow.

2

Do not flush

If the toilet will flush at all, the water has nowhere to go and may rise in the tub.

3

Find the cleanout cap

It is often near the house wall or the property line. Leave it closed if the line is full, since opening it can release a rush of sewage.

4

Protect floors and belongings

Move rugs and boxes away from the lowest drain, and keep children and pets clear.

If the backup may come from the City main and not your private line, the City directs residents to Police Dispatch at 707-421-7373, which is staffed 24/7.

How a Main Line Is Cleared

Main line work uses bigger tools than a sink cable and often includes a quick look inside the pipe.

1

Open the cleanout

The plumber caps off the area, opens the cleanout and releases built-up pressure before anything goes in.

2

Run a large cable machine

A heavy-duty cable with a cutting head breaks through the blockage and trims roots from the pipe wall.

3

Scour if the line is coated

When the cable leaves grease or scale behind, hydro jetting washes the walls clean.

4

Confirm with water and, if needed, a camera

Water runs through the line to prove it moves. A camera check may follow to find out why it blocked.

Tides, Storms and Backups in Suisun City

Coverage of the 2022 Flood Walk describes flooding downtown and inland, with king tides, storm surge and sea level rise named as drivers. Heavy water outside can overload mains and push into private laterals, so backups are more likely during wet spells. If your drains slow after a storm, that timing is a clue worth telling the plumber.

Soft marsh ground adds to the picture. Pipe in settling soil can sag over time, and a sag holds solids that a cleaning removes only for a while. The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), includes provisions on backwater valves, so ask the plumber whether one suits your lowest fixtures.

Source: Daily Republic coverage of the September 24, 2022 Flood Walk

Whose Line Is It, and Who Should You Call?

The City owns about 70 miles of smaller mains, and the Fairfield-Suisun Sewer District owns the trunk mains and the treatment plant. The pipe from your building to the main is the property owner's job. A plumber can tell you where your lateral ends.

If the blockage is in your lateral, call the 24/7 plumbing line. If you think it is in the City main, call Police Dispatch at 707-421-7373. Neighbors outside the city can read about Fairfield service and Vacaville service.

What Affects the Price of Sewer Line Cleaning

The price depends on the line and the blockage, and it is stated before work starts.

Cleanout access. A cap at ground level is quick. If none exists, the plumber may need to work through a toilet or roof vent.
Length of line. A long run from house to main takes more cable and time.
Nature of the blockage. Roots or packed debris need more effort than a soft clog.
Added steps. Jetting or a camera run after clearing adds scope.
