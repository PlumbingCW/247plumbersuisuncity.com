How to Replace a Tankless Water Heater in Suisun City, CA

To replace a tankless water heater, a plumber shuts off gas or power and water, isolates and drains the unit, disconnects the vent and lines, mounts the new heater, and tests everything. A like-for-like swap is simpler than a first install, but gas, venting, and flow settings still need a licensed hand.

By Suisun City 24/7 Plumbing | Published October 15, 2026 | Updated October 15, 2026

Repair, Flush, or Replace? Start With the Symptoms

Not every cold-water complaint means a dead heater. Scale in the heat exchanger, a clogged inlet screen, or a failed sensor can mimic a unit that is finished.

A technician reads the error code, checks flow and gas pressure, and tells you which path makes sense. If the heat exchanger has failed or repairs keep recurring, replacement usually wins.

Warning Signs a Tankless Unit Is Near the End

These clues point toward replacement rather than another repair.

Cold bursts mid-shower

Water runs hot, then cold, then hot again even after a cleaning. The exchanger may be too scaled to hold temperature.

Repeated error codes

The same fault returns after each reset or repair. The display is flagging a part that no longer works reliably.

Leaks at the unit

Water dripping from the cabinet or under the heater often means a cracked exchanger or a failed internal seal.

Rising gas use

The burner runs longer to deliver the same heat. Scale insulates the exchanger and wastes fuel.

What Happens During a Replacement Visit

A same-fuel swap on a clean wall mount takes the least time. Changes in size, fuel, or vent type add work.

1

Isolate and drain

Gas or power is shut off, the isolation valves are closed, and the unit is drained so the lines can be disconnected safely.

2

Remove the old heater

Vent, gas, and water connections come off. The plumber checks the wall mount and the condition of the vent pipe.

3

Mount and connect

The new unit is fastened to the wall, then gas, water, vent, and condensate lines are hooked up. Isolation valves are added if the old setup lacked them.

4

Fire up and set flow

The plumber purges air, lights the burner, tests every joint, and confirms the temperature setting and flow rate.

Safety Steps If Your Tankless Unit Acts Up

Handle these in order before anyone touches the unit.

  • ✓
    Smell gas? Leave. Get everyone outside, do not flip switches, and call 911 from outside before you call a plumber.
  • ✓
    Treat venting trouble as a CO risk. A loose, disconnected, or sooty vent can send exhaust into the house. Open windows, leave if anyone feels dizzy or sick, and keep the unit off.
  • ✓
    Watch for scalding water. A faulty sensor can send water far hotter than the setting. Test with your hand before bathing, and keep children away.
  • ✓
    Know what to shut off. Close the gas valve to the unit and the cold water isolation valve. For an electric model, switch off its breaker.
  • ✓
    Do not remove covers. Leave internal parts to a licensed technician. Call the 24/7 line and describe the error code.

Choosing the Next Unit Before the Old One Quits

Start with how much hot water you need at the same moment. Two showers and a running dishwasher call for more flow than one shower alone, and tankless ratings are about flow, not tank size.

Next, look at what the old unit taught you. If it struggled when two people showered, size up. If the family shrank, a smaller unit may do the job and save fuel.

Think about the fuel and the vent

A new unit needs the same fuel, or a conversion that your plumber has to plan. Venting is the detail that surprises people, since newer models often use different pipe and routing. Ask your plumber to confirm the vent material and routing match both the maker's instructions and the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Ask about the first-year routine

Request a simple maintenance schedule in writing. A flush on the right interval is the simplest way to protect a unit in hard water and keep its flow steady.

Homeowner Chores and Pro-Only Steps

A few tasks are easy to do yourself, and others carry real risk.

Reasonable to try yourself

  • Cleaning the inlet screen
  • Reading the display code and writing it down
  • Clearing space around the unit
  • Shutting off gas and water in an emergency

Better left to a plumber

  • Gas line work and leak testing
  • Venting changes and condensate drains
  • Flow and temperature calibration
  • Diagnosing a failed exchanger
  • Anchoring the unit to the wall

Tankless heaters are less forgiving of poor venting or low gas supply than a storage tank. If you are sizing a new unit, our water heater installation team can check gas supply and vent runs on the spot.

Planning Around a Few Days Without Hot Water

A planned swap rarely leaves you without hot water for long, but gas or venting surprises can stretch the job. Schedule it on a day when you are home and the area around the unit is clear. Our tankless water heater technicians can confirm flow and vent needs before the day you schedule.

If the unit fails first, you still have options. Our water heater replacement team handles emergency swaps around the clock, including tank units if that suits your home better.
