Plumbing Leak in Slab: Suisun City, CA Homeowner Guide

A plumbing leak in a slab usually shows up as a higher water bill, the sound of running water with every tap off, or a warm patch on the floor. Shut off the water at the main, and if the sound stops, call a licensed plumber to locate the leak before it spreads.

By Suisun City 24/7 Plumbing | Published October 15, 2026 | Updated October 15, 2026

Four Clues That Water Is Escaping Under Your Floor

A leak under concrete hides well, but the house usually gives hints long before the floor fails.

A bill that climbs for no reason

Your usage jumps while the household routine stays the same. A steady drip under the slab runs all day and all night, so the meter keeps turning.

Running water sounds

You hear a faint hiss or trickle in a quiet house, even with every fixture off. The sound is often loudest near the floor.

Warm or damp spots

A hot-water line that leaks can warm a patch of tile or carpet. Damp flooring, a musty smell, or lifted laminate points the same way.

Low pressure or cracks

Weak flow at several taps, new gaps along baseboards, or hairline cracks in the floor can all trace back to water working under the concrete.

A Ten-Minute Meter Test Before You Call

This check tells you whether water is leaving your plumbing system. It does not tell you where.

1

Stop all water use

Turn off the dishwasher, washer, ice maker, and irrigation. Make sure nobody flushes or runs a tap.

2

Read the meter

Open the meter box and look at the leak indicator, usually a small triangle or dial. If it spins with everything off, water is escaping somewhere.

3

Close your main valve

Shut the house main and check the meter again. If it stops, the leak is on your side of the meter. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house.

4

Note what you find

Write down the meter reading, the time, and any warm spots or sounds. That record helps the plumber narrow the search quickly.

What You Can Check Yourself and What Needs Equipment

Homeowners can confirm a problem. Pinpointing it is a different job.

Safe to do on your own

  • Read the meter and run the shutoff test
  • Walk barefoot over floors to feel for warm spots
  • Listen at floors and walls in a quiet house
  • Check the yard for soggy ground near the water line
  • Shut off the main to limit damage

Leave to a licensed plumber

  • Pinpointing the leak with acoustic or thermal tools
  • Pressure testing isolated sections of pipe
  • Cutting into the slab or flooring
  • Rerouting or replacing pipe
  • Drying out and checking for hidden moisture

The costly mistake is guessing with a jackhammer. Locating the leak first keeps the opening small and the repair focused. A leak detection visit is usually the right first step once your meter test points to a hidden leak.

What Happens When the Plumber Arrives

The visit starts with questions. Where did you hear the water, when did the bill change, and which rooms feel warm? Those answers shape the test plan.

Next comes a pressure check on the hot and cold lines, run separately. If one side loses pressure and the other holds, the search area shrinks fast. Sound and thermal tools then trace the pipe path to the likely spot.

Marking the leak, not the whole floor

Once the leak is located, the plumber marks a small area and explains the options. You hear what each method involves, how much flooring is affected, and how long the water stays off.

Your say on the method

A good plumber lays out the choices and lets you decide. A single failed fitting in sound pipe calls for a different plan than a line that has pinholes in three places. Any new pipe should meet the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and pressure testing is how the plumber shows it holds.

Why Waiting Makes the Damage Spread

A slab leak does not heal. Water softens the soil under the concrete, feeds mold in the flooring above, and can wear away more pipe the longer it runs. Wet insulation and baseboards add repair work that has nothing to do with the pipe.
