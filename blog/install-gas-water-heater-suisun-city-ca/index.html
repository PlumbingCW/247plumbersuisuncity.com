How to Install a Gas Water Heater: Suisun City, CA Guide

Installing a gas water heater means shutting off gas and water, draining the old tank, setting the new unit, connecting gas, water, and the vent, then testing for leaks and proper draft. The gas and venting steps carry real risk, so most homeowners hire a licensed plumber for them.

By Suisun City 24/7 Plumbing | Published October 15, 2026 | Updated October 15, 2026

Weekend Project or Plumber's Job?

A gas water heater mixes fuel, fire, exhaust, and hot water in one appliance. That is why the line between DIY and professional work matters more here than on most home projects.

Tasks homeowners often handle

  • Clearing space around the old heater
  • Turning off the gas valve and the cold supply
  • Draining a cooled tank through a hose
  • Hauling the old unit away
  • Choosing a tank size that fits your household

Tasks that call for a licensed plumber

  • Disconnecting and reconnecting the gas line
  • Testing every gas joint for leaks
  • Sizing and setting the vent so exhaust leaves safely
  • Installing the relief valve and its discharge line
  • Anchoring the tank and confirming proper draft

If any gas smell shows up, leave the house and call 911 before you call a plumber. Then reach our team through the 24/7 line once everyone is safe.

How the Installation Runs, Start to Finish

Here is the order a plumber works in. Knowing it helps you judge a quote and ask good questions.

1

Shut off and drain

Gas and cold water are closed, power to any igniter is checked, and the old tank is drained to a safe spot. Hot water from the tank can scald, so it cools first.

2

Remove the old unit

The gas line, water connectors, and vent are disconnected, and the heater is moved out. The plumber inspects the vent pipe and shutoff valves for wear while access is open.

3

Set, connect, and brace

The new tank goes on a level base with the connectors, relief valve, and gas line attached. Straps or anchors secure it against earthquake motion.

4

Fill, light, and test

The tank fills before the burner lights, so it never fires dry. Every gas joint is checked for leaks, and the plumber confirms exhaust is drawing up the vent.

Local note

California's Bracing Rule for Water Heaters

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced, anchored, or strapped so an earthquake cannot knock them over. It also covers existing residential units, and sellers certify compliance to buyers.

For a Suisun City homeowner, that means the strapping is part of the job, not an upgrade. A heater that tips can break its gas and water connections at once, so insist the bracing is done and ask the plumber to show you.

Source: California Health and Safety Code section 19211, applying to standard units of 120 gallons or less.

Check These Before the Installer Arrives

A little preparation keeps the visit short and the price accurate.

  • ✓
    Locate the gas shutoff. Everyone in the house should know where it is and how to turn it.
  • ✓
    Clear the area. Move boxes, paint, and anything flammable well away from the heater.
  • ✓
    Note the tank details. Read the capacity and fuel type on the label, and take a photo of the vent setup.
  • ✓

  • ✓

Mistakes That Cause Trouble After an Install

Most problems come from a few skipped details, not from the heater itself.

Venting that sags or leaks

Exhaust must rise and exit cleanly. A loose or flat vent run can let combustion gas drift back into the room.

Missing relief valve line

The temperature and pressure valve needs a proper discharge pipe. Without it, a release can scald someone standing nearby.

Dry firing the burner

Lighting the burner before the tank is full can damage the tank. Always fill first and bleed air from a hot tap.

Skipping the leak test

Soap solution or a gas detector at each joint is the check. Trusting your nose alone leaves small leaks undiscovered.

Matching Tank Size to Your Household

These are general rules of thumb, not a sizing calculation. Your plumber can refine them for your usage.

HouseholdTypical tank rangeNotes
One to two people30 to 40 gallonsFine for modest showers and few back-to-back uses
Three to four people40 to 50 gallonsCommon choice for a family home
Five or more people50 gallons and upConsider recovery speed, not just tank size
Heavy laundry or soaking tubsStep up one sizePeak demand matters more than the average

Recovery rate, the speed a heater reheats after a draw, often matters as much as capacity.

Why Hard Water Shapes Your New Heater's Life

Suisun City's 2025 water quality report lists hardness at 170 ppm, about 9.9 grains per gallon. On the common USGS scale, that falls in the hard band.

Hard water leaves scale on the tank bottom and heating surfaces, which makes a gas burner work harder and can shorten the heater's life. Draining the tank on a schedule helps, and a water softener or filtration system protects the unit and the pipes feeding it.

What a Professional Install Adds That a Kit Cannot

A kit gives you parts. It does not give you a trained eye for a rusted vent, a failing shutoff valve, or a gas line that needs a new fitting. Those are the findings that turn a quick swap into a safe one.

Professionals also carry the right fittings and test gear, which cuts return trips. Gas work is not forgiving

A small leak at a gas joint will not always announce itself. Plumbers test joints with proper solution or a detector, because smell alone is not a safe check.
