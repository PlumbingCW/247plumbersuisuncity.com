Free Water Leak Detection at Home: Suisun City, CA Guide

To check for a hidden leak yourself, shut off every tap and appliance, read your water meter, wait an hour without using water, and read it again. If the numbers changed or the leak indicator is spinning, water is escaping somewhere. This is a do-it-yourself test, and it tells you whether to keep looking or call a plumber.

By Suisun City 24/7 Plumbing | Published October 15, 2026 | Updated October 15, 2026

The One-Hour Water Meter Test

You need a flashlight, a pen, and an hour when nobody is home using water.

1

Stop all water use

Turn off faucets, the dishwasher, the washing machine, and the ice maker. Make sure no one flushes a toilet during the test. If you have an irrigation system, pause it.

2

Find and open the meter box

The meter usually sits in a lidded box near the curb or the front of the property. Lift the lid carefully, since insects and debris collect inside. Wipe the glass clean.

3

Check the leak indicator

Most meters have a small triangle or star-shaped dial that spins when any water moves through. If it turns with everything off, you have a leak right now. Write down the main reading either way.

4

Wait, then compare

Leave everything off for an hour and read the meter again. Any change in the number, or a dial that moved, confirms water is leaving the system.

What the Result Tells You

No change at all means the supply pipes are probably holding. A leak that only runs when something is in use, such as a toilet that refills after flushing, can still exist, so keep reading the sections below.

If the dial moved

Now you know the leak is somewhere between the meter and a fixture. The next job is narrowing it down. Do not assume the first wet spot you see is the source, because water travels along pipes and framing before it shows up.

If the meter was still

Run the test again after a night when the household has not used water for several hours, so even a very slow leak has time to register. A tiny drip at a fixture may take longer to show up on the dial than a broken pipe will.

Narrowing Down the Source

Once the meter says there is a leak, work through the likely culprits in this order.

  • ✓
    Toilets first. Drop a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.
  • ✓
    Dripping faucets and showerheads. A single drip per second wastes a surprising amount over a day. Look under sinks too, where supply lines and shutoff valves weep.
  • ✓
    Outdoor spigots and irrigation. Check hose bibs, sprinkler valves, and any drip line for wet soil or pooled water near the house.
  • ✓
    Water heater and softener connections. Look at fittings, the base of the tank, and any relief valve discharge pipe for moisture or rust streaks.
  • ✓
    Floors, walls, and ceilings. Warm spots on a floor, bubbling paint, a musty smell, or sounds of running water when everything is off suggest a hidden pipe leak.

DIY Checks Versus Professional Tracing

A meter test is a good first step. Some situations call for more than that.

SituationWhat you can doWhen to call a plumber
Toilet runs after flushingDye test, replace flapperTank or fill valve still leaks after new parts
Meter moves, no visible waterCheck fixtures and outdoor linesNo leak found after one pass
Warm spot on a floorShut off water to the house if it growsRight away, since this can mean a slab line
High bill, no change in habitsRun the meter testIf the test is positive and you cannot find the source
Wet drywall or ceiling stainStop water use in that areaAlways, to avoid mold and further damage

Why Hidden Leaks Matter

Water moves along the easiest path, so the damp spot is rarely above the break. A leaking joint in a wall can run down studs and show up in a baseboard a room away. Left alone, it feeds mold, rots framing, and wastes water on every billing cycle. Any repair made after the test should meet California Contractors State License Board standards for licensed work, which you can confirm with the CSLB License Check tool.

Leaks under a concrete slab are harder to catch because nothing drips where you can see it. If the meter is moving and you notice a warm floor or the sound of running water in a quiet house, a slab leak repair specialist may need to confirm the location before anyone digs. Any repair to a supply line should meet the California Plumbing Code, so check the plumber's license with the CSLB License Check tool before you agree to work.

Limit the Damage While You Decide

If the test is positive and you cannot find the source, close the shutoff valve to any fixture you suspect and leave the rest of the house running. Know where your main shutoff valve is before you need it, and make sure everyone in the house can find it.

Take a photo of the meter reading and note the time. That record helps a plumber work out how fast water is escaping, which narrows the search. Move belongings away from any damp wall or floor, and keep a towel and bucket handy if a fitting is dripping.

Fixing It Yourself or Bringing in Help

Some leaks are an afternoon project. Others are not.

Within reach for most homeowners

  • Replacing a toilet flapper or fill valve
  • Swapping a worn washer in a faucet
  • Tightening a loose supply line nut
  • Replacing a cracked hose washer

Better left to a plumber

  • Any leak inside a wall or ceiling
  • A leak in the line between the meter and the house
  • Suspected slab leaks
  • Pressure problems that appear along with a leak

If the line from the meter to your house is the problem, water line repair is a separate job from fixing a fixture. A plumber will give you a clear price before work starts.
