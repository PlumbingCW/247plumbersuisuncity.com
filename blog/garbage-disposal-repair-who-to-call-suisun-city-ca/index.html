Who to Call for Garbage Disposal Repair in Suisun City, CA

Call a plumber for a garbage disposal that leaks, jams, hums without spinning, or backs water into the sink. Call an electrician only if the unit is dead and the breaker and wall switch check out fine. Start with the quick checks below, since many jams clear in a few minutes with an Allen wrench.

By Suisun City 24/7 Plumbing | Published October 15, 2026 | Updated October 15, 2026

Match the Symptom to the Right Pro

A garbage disposal sits where plumbing and electrical work meet, which is why homeowners wonder who to call. The answer depends on what the unit is doing. Water problems go to a plumber. Power problems go to an electrician only after the simple checks are done.

Most failures are mechanical or drain-related, so a plumber is the right first call in the majority of cases. A licensed plumber also sets the trap and drain connections to the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

If you smell burning or see sparks, turn off the breaker first and do not touch the unit.

Four Checks Before You Pick Up the Phone

These take about ten minutes and often solve the problem.

1

Cut the power

Switch off the wall switch and unplug the unit if it has a plug under the sink. Never put a hand in a disposal, even when it is off.

2

Unjam the flywheel

Insert the Allen wrench that came with the unit into the hex socket on the bottom and work it back and forth until it turns smoothly. Pull out any object you can see with tongs.

3

Press the reset button

Wait a few minutes for the motor to cool, then push the red reset button on the bottom of the unit. It trips when the motor overheats or jams.

4

Run cold water and test

Restore power, run cold water, and flip the switch. If it spins, grind a few ice cubes to knock off residue. If it hums or stays silent, the unit needs a closer look.

Repair or Replace the Unit

Disposals are not built to last forever, and a repair that costs more in time than a replacement is not a good use of money.

Repair makes sense when

The unit is fairly new, the leak is at a seal or a loose connection, or the jam cleared and the motor runs strong. These fixes are fast, and the rest of the unit usually still has years left.

Replacement makes sense when

The housing is cracked, the motor is burnt out, or the flange leaks after you reseat it. A unit that jams often, runs weak, or has rust streaks near the base is also near the end. Our plumbers handle garbage disposal repair and replacement, and give a clear price before work starts.

What Never Belongs in a Disposal

Most calls trace back to something that should have gone in the trash.

  • ✓
    Grease and cooking oil. It cools in the drain and builds a coating that catches everything else.
  • ✓
    Fibrous food. Celery, corn husks, artichokes, and onion skins wrap around the blades.
  • ✓
    Starches. Rice, pasta, and potato peels swell with water and turn to paste.
  • ✓
    Bones and pits. Large pieces jam the flywheel and can damage the motor.
  • ✓
    Coffee grounds in volume. A spoonful goes down fine, but a full filter packs into sludge in the trap.

When the Drain Is the Real Problem

If the disposal spins fine but the sink fills with water, the clog is downstream. A disposal does not clear a blocked kitchen drain, and running it harder only pushes debris deeper. In that case, you need drain cleaning rather than a new disposal.

Look at the dishwasher too. Many dishwashers drain into the disposal, so a blockage there can back dirty water into the dishwasher tub. If both the sink and the dishwasher misbehave, treat it as one drain problem. Dishwasher drain hookups, including the air gap or high loop, follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) standards behind the California Plumbing Code.

Habits That Keep the Blades Turning

Run a strong stream of cold water before, during, and for about 15 seconds after grinding. Cold water keeps grease solid so it gets chopped up and flushed out instead of smearing the pipe walls. Warm water melts fat into the line, where it hardens again farther down.

Feed scraps in small amounts instead of one big handful. A crowded chamber strains the motor and packs food against the baffle. Every few weeks, grind a handful of ice and a few citrus peels to scrub the chamber and freshen the smell.

Listen to the unit. A new rattle, a slower spin, or a drain that gurgles after use are early signs of trouble. Catching them early usually means a small repair rather than a midnight emergency.

Local note

Who Owns the Pipe Behind Your Sink

The City of Suisun City owns and operates about 70 miles of smaller sewer mains. The pipe that runs from your house to the main, called the lateral, belongs to the property owner. The Fairfield-Suisun Sewer District says it does not maintain or repair private sewer laterals.

That matters for a kitchen drain that keeps backing up after a disposal repair. If a plumber finds the blockage is in the lateral, the cleaning is the owner's responsibility. For a sewer backup, the City tells residents to call Police Dispatch right away so the right crew is sent.

Source: City of Suisun City Sewer and Wastewater page and the Fairfield-Suisun Sewer District FAQ.
