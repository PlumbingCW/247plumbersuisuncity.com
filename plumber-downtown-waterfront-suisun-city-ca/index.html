The Downtown Waterfront is Suisun City's commercial and mixed-use district along Main Street and the marina. This page is for shops, restaurants, offices and apartments, not single-family homes.

What the Downtown Waterfront Is

The Downtown Waterfront is a commercial and mixed-use district, not a residential neighborhood. The City's Downtown and Waterfront Specific Plan covers Main Street, the Town Square, the marina and the area around the historic train depot.

Main Street Commercial in the plan allows ground-floor shops, restaurants and salons, with apartments and condominiums as conditional uses. A single block can hold a kitchen, a retail space and a home upstairs, all on related lines.

Plumbing for Restaurants and Shops

Food service places depend on grease-laden drains, floor drains and hot water recovery. Tell the dispatcher what the business does so the plumber can bring the right gear.

Typical downtown calls

Kitchen and floor drains that back up are cleared with drain cleaning, and repeat grease clogs call for hydro jetting. Restrooms, mechanical rooms and large piping fall under commercial plumbing.

Grease handling rules and any equipment requirements vary, so ask the City before you change a kitchen layout.

Local note

Downtown's Flood and Soft-Ground Setting

Suisun City sits about 6.6 feet above sea level beside Suisun Marsh. A 2022 Flood Walk with the City and the Bay Conservation and Development Commission described the downtown as having flooded more than once, with king tides and storm surge named as drivers.

For a business owner, that means checking floor drains and sump or backflow devices before the wet season and writing down when slow drains coincide with high water. A leak detection visit helps when a slab or wall is damp with no obvious source.

Sources: Daily Republic coverage of the 2022 Flood Walk, City of Suisun City.

Older Buildings and Mixed Use

Downtown was rebuilt after the fire of July 9, 1888, so some commercial buildings predate modern plumbing. We do not assume what any building contains, and a sewer inspection shows what the lateral looks like inside.

In a building with apartments above a shop, the water heater and sewer lateral may serve both. Know your shutoff valves before an emergency, and tell the dispatcher who else is on the line.

Sewer Backups Downtown

Downtown sewer service is shared between the City, which owns the smaller mains, and the Fairfield-Suisun Sewer District, which owns the large trunk lines. The lateral from your building to the main belongs to the property owner.

For an active backup the City asks residents to call Police Dispatch at 707-421-7373. Keep the water off in the building until a plumber has checked your lateral.
