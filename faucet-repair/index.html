A Dripping Faucet Is Slow Water Damage

A drip looks harmless, but it keeps wetting the same spot on the sink, the cabinet floor or the tub. That moisture stains the finish, loosens caulk and can soften a particleboard vanity from the inside.

The cause is usually a worn washer, seat or cartridge, and the repair is small if it is done early. Left alone, the metal seat wears into a groove and a simple fix turns into a faucet replacement.

Where the Faucet Problem Lives: Kitchen, Bath, Shower and Yard

The same drip means different things depending on where it appears.

  • ✓
    Kitchen spout. Drips from the tip, a loose base or a pull-down hose that leaks under the sink.
  • ✓
    Bathroom sink. Slow drips, weak flow from a clogged aerator and handles that feel loose or stiff.
  • ✓
    Shower or tub valve. Water that keeps running after the handle is off, or a temperature that swings when someone flushes.
  • ✓
    Under-sink stop valves. Small shutoffs that seep at the stem or will not turn. They matter because every other repair depends on them.
  • ✓
    Outdoor hose bibs. Drips at the handle or water inside the wall when the hose is connected.

If the leak is inside a wall or you hear water running with every tap closed, the problem is bigger than a faucet. Our leak detection service finds hidden leaks without opening up the whole room.

Faucet or Shower Valve Won't Shut Off? Do This First

We pick up 24/7. Until the plumber arrives, these steps cap the flow.

1

Use the stop valve

Turn the oval handles under the sink clockwise. Hand-tight is enough. Forcing a stuck valve can snap the stem.

2

No stop valve? Go to the main

Find the main water valve for the house and close it. Open a low faucet afterward to relieve pressure.

3

Catch the water

Put a bucket under the leak and towels around the cabinet. Move cleaning products out of the way.

4

Do not pry the handle

A broken handle can leave the cartridge exposed. Keep it as is and describe it to the dispatcher.

If water sprays from a pipe in the wall, treat it as a burst pipe and shut the main immediately.

Safety First: Before You Touch a Faucet

Most faucet jobs are safe for a careful homeowner. These points keep a small repair from turning into a bigger one.

  • ✓
    Shut off the supply. Close the stop valves under the sink, or the main, and open the faucet to confirm the flow has stopped.
  • ✓
    Watch for scalding. Water from the heater can burn. Let a hot line cool before you open a cartridge, and test the temperature before anyone steps into the shower.
  • ✓
    Keep electricity away from water. If water is near an outlet, a disposal cord or a panel, stay clear and shut breakers only with dry hands from a dry spot.
  • ✓
    Know when to stop. Stop if a stem will not turn, a handle snaps, a shutoff will not hold or you cannot find the leak. Call the 24/7 line before it gets worse.

Washer, Cartridge or Whole Faucet?

Three designs cover almost every faucet in a home, and each wears out in its own way.

Compression faucets

These have two handles and a rubber washer that presses on a seat. When the washer hardens, the faucet drips. Replacing the washer and dressing the seat usually fixes it.

Cartridge and ball faucets

Single-handle faucets use a sealed cartridge or a ball assembly. Water seeping past it means the seals are worn. A new cartridge cures it as long as the housing is not scored.

Ceramic disc faucets

These last longest but crack if scale builds up between the discs. Replacement of the disc set is the repair, and it is not a quick DIY task.

What 170 ppm Does to Aerators and Cartridges

Suisun City's 2025 water quality report lists hardness at 170 ppm, near the top of the hard band on the common USGS scale. The minerals leave white crust on spouts and grit inside cartridges.

So an aerator that clogs every few months and a cartridge that wears quickly are not unusual here. If you replace parts often, ask about water softener and filtration options when the plumber is on site. The California Plumbing Code, which follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), expects a new faucet to have proper air gaps and backflow protection on its spout and hose, so a replacement should match those rules.

Repair the Faucet or Replace It?

Parts are easy to swap on a good faucet. On a worn one, you may chase leaks for months.

Repair makes sense when

  • The faucet is in good shape and parts are available
  • The leak is at the spout or handle
  • The finish is clean and undamaged
  • You like the style and height

Replace makes sense when

  • The body is corroded or pitted
  • Parts are discontinued
  • The base wobbles or the deck is cracked
  • You are changing the sink or counter anyway

If you pick replacement, the new fixture is set up the same visit. A larger remodel with added lines is covered under plumbing installation.
