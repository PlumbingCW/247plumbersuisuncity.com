Four Toilet Problems and What Each One Usually Means

Most toilet calls start with one of these symptoms. Matching the symptom to the likely cause saves a lot of guessing.

Water around the base

A failed wax ring or loose closet bolts let water escape at the floor. The leak is often slow, so a spongy floor or a stain below is the first clue.

Runs after every flush

A worn flapper, a bent chain or a fill valve that will not shut off keeps water moving into the bowl all night.

Bowl rises, then drains slowly

A partial clog in the trap or just past it. If it keeps happening, the cause may sit farther down the line.

Rocks when you sit

Loose bolts or a damaged flange. Leave it alone too long and the movement tears the seal.

Toilet Overflowing? Do This Before We Arrive

We answer 24/7. These steps keep the bathroom floor dry while the plumber is on the way.

1

Close the supply valve

Turn the small oval handle on the wall or floor behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.

2

Lift the tank lid

Push the rubber flapper down into its seat. That stops water from refilling the bowl.

3

Do not flush again

Each flush on a blocked toilet adds more water to the floor.

4

Protect the floor

Lay towels along the edge and keep children and pets out of the wet room.

5

Skip the chemicals

Drain chemicals sit in the bowl and splash back on the plumber's hands. Tell us if you already used any.

If every drain in the house is gurgling, close the toilet lid, stop using water and tell the dispatcher. That is usually a main line problem, not just the toilet.

What to Try Yourself and When to Hand It Over

A few fixes are fine for a homeowner. Others make a small leak worse.

Reasonable to try

  • Plunge with a flange plunger and steady strokes
  • Swap a worn flapper
  • Tighten the supply line by hand
  • Adjust the float so the tank stops at the fill line

Time to call a plumber

  • Water on the floor with no clear source
  • A rocking toilet or a cracked bowl
  • Repeat clogs within days of plunging
  • Gurgling in a nearby tub or sink
  • Any sewage smell or backing up in other fixtures

The line between the two is whether you can see the cause. If you can't, extra turns on a bolt or more plunging usually do more harm than good.

The Wax Ring and Why Floors Stay Wet

The wax ring sits between the toilet and the drain flange. When it fails, the flush water seeps out under the base instead of going down the pipe. You may only notice a damp baseboard, a soft vinyl patch or a stain on the ceiling below.

Fixing it means lifting the toilet, replacing the ring, checking the flange and setting the toilet back level. If the floor around the flange has rotted, the toilet has nothing solid to sit on. We will tell you that before we reinstall it. The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), sets how much clearance a toilet needs around it, so a reset that leaves it crowded against a wall or tub may need to be moved.

Hard Water Wears Out Toilet Parts Faster

The City's 2025 water quality report lists tap water at 170 ppm hardness. That is on the hard side of the common USGS scale, and it leaves mineral crust on fill valves and flapper seats.

A crusted flapper no longer seals, so the tank trickles into the bowl all night. If you replace the same part every year or two, scale is the likely reason, and a look at water softener and filtration options may be worth your time.

When the Problem Is Past the Toilet

A single clogged toilet is a fixture problem. A toilet that bubbles when the washer drains, or a tub that fills when you flush, is a line problem. The blockage is shared by several fixtures, and plunging will not clear it.

In that case we cable the line from a cleanout or through the toilet. If it keeps closing, a camera run shows whether roots or a sag are behind it, and our sewer line cleaning service covers the main.

What Changes the Price of a Toilet Repair

A flapper swap and a floor-level reset are not the same job. These factors set the number.

Part or whole toilet. Small internal parts cost little. A full replacement adds the fixture and disposal of the old one.
Floor condition. Soft wood under the flange needs repair before the toilet can go back.
Clog depth. A blockage in the trap clears fast. One in the branch line takes more time.
Time of the call. Night and weekend visits carry a premium compared to a scheduled day.
