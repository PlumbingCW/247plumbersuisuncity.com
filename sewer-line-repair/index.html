Local note

Who Owns Which Part of the Sewer Line

The City of Suisun City runs about 70 miles of smaller gravity sewer mains, and the Fairfield-Suisun Sewer District owns the larger trunk mains and the treatment plant. Neither agency maintains the private lateral, the pipe that runs from your building out to the main. That pipe is the property owner's responsibility, including repair.

In practice this means a cracked lateral on your lot is yours to fix, while a failure out at the main is a City matter. The plumber can tell you which side of the connection the problem is on, and the Sewer District can confirm that a lateral ties into the public main.

Source: City of Suisun City Sewer and Wastewater page and the Fairfield-Suisun Sewer District FAQ

Sewage Coming Up? Do This First

A failed lateral can send wastewater into the house or the yard. These steps limit the harm.

1

Stop using water

Every flush and every load of laundry adds to the backup. Turn off the dishwasher and washer.

2

Keep clear of the spill

Sewage carries bacteria. Keep children, pets and bare hands away, and wash well after any contact.

3

Call the City if the main may be involved

For sewer failures the City directs residents to Police Dispatch at 707-421-7373, which is staffed around the clock. Dispatch can send the right crew.

4

Note what you see

A wet patch in the yard, a sunken spot or water at a cleanout all help the plumber find the break.

Do not pour bleach or chemical cleaner into a line you suspect is broken. It will not fix a crack and can irritate anyone who handles the pipe later.

Signs the Pipe Itself Is Failing

A clog can be cleared. A broken pipe has to be repaired, and the clues are different.

Backups return after cleaning

Roots or a belly in the pipe catch debris again and again. The line opens for a while, then jams.

Sewer smell in the yard

Gas can leak out through a cracked pipe. A foul odor near one spot often marks the break.

Green, soggy or sunken ground

Leaking wastewater feeds grass and softens soil. A dip over the line can mean soil has washed into the pipe.

Slow drains in more than one room

A damaged section low in the line holds water back. Sinks, tubs and toilets all slow together.

What Wears Out a Lateral

Roots are the usual suspect. They enter at joints seeking moisture and swell until the pipe cracks. Clay and older concrete pipe have many joints, so they are easy targets.

Settling ground

Local history notes that Suisun City was built on marshy land, and the old railroad bed across the marsh sank six to 12 inches a year until fill and drainage fixed it. Soil that moves, even slowly, can pull pipe joints apart or create low spots that hold water. For a buried lateral, that means problems can start without any sign at the surface.

Age and material

Much of the housing stock grew between the late 1970s and the 1990s, since the population rose from about 2,900 in 1970 to about 22,700 in 1990. Lines that old can show cracked clay, corroded cast iron or belled joints. A camera run shows which one you have.

What Shapes the Price of a Sewer Repair

Sewer work varies a lot, so the price depends on the situation.

Length of damaged pipe. A short spot repair costs less than relining or replacing a long run.
Depth and location. A pipe under concrete, landscaping or a driveway takes more work to reach.
Method chosen. Trenchless options can reduce digging but are not right for every pipe.
Permits and inspections. Rules vary, so ask the Suisun City Building Division whether your job needs a permit.

Licensing and Permits Before You Sign

Hire a licensed contractor and verify the license yourself. Look up any plumber with the Contractors State License Board at cslb.ca.gov or by phone at (800) 321-2752. A license number on a flyer does not prove the license is active. Repaired pipe is expected to meet the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including steady slope and a usable cleanout.

Permit rules vary by job type. Contact the Suisun City Building Division at 707-421-7317 and check current counter hours before work begins. If you want an overview of the whole network, the about page explains how calls are handled.
