Suisun City tap water measured 170 ppm hardness in the City's 2025 report, about 9.9 grains per gallon. That is the white crust on your faucets, and it is also what shortens the life of your water heater.

What 170 ppm Means in Your Kitchen and Bathroom

Hardness is a measure of dissolved calcium and magnesium. Dividing 170 ppm by 17.1 gives about 9.9 grains per gallon, which is the unit softener makers use for sizing.

On the common USGS scale, 121 to 180 ppm is hard and anything above 180 is very hard. Suisun City water sits in the hard band, near the top of it, so the effects are easy to see.

The same report lists total dissolved solids at 220 ppm. Hardness is only part of that number, and it is the part that leaves scale on pipes, glass and heating elements.

Signs of Hard Water Around the House

Most homeowners notice the symptoms long before they connect them to the water.

White crust on fixtures

Chalky buildup around faucet spouts and showerheads is dried calcium. It returns a few days after cleaning.

Spots on glasses and dishes

Cloudy film and spotting after the dishwasher runs is hard water drying on the surface.

Dry skin and stiff laundry

Soap reacts with minerals and leaves a film. Towels feel rough and soap does not lather easily.

Noisy, slow water heater

Scale on elements and sediment on the tank floor shrink hot water supply and shorten the heater's life.

Sizing a Softener for Your Household

A softener is sized by how many grains of hardness it must remove between regenerations. Take the number of people in the house, multiply by typical daily water use and then by the 9.9 grains per gallon in the City's water.

A unit that is too small regenerates constantly and wastes salt. One that is too large can sit too long between cycles and cause stale water in the resin.

Bring up bathrooms, laundry habits and any irrigation you want kept off the softener. The plumber uses those details to pick a size that matches how your household really uses water. To talk it through, contact the dispatch team.

Taste and Chlorine Are a Separate Question

The City's treated water is disinfected with chlorine. Some people notice a chlorine taste or smell, and a softener does not touch that.

A carbon filter at the kitchen tap or at the point where the water enters the house handles taste and odor. If your complaint is flavor rather than scale, that is the simpler and lower-cost route to ask about first.

Households that want both scale control and better-tasting water often pair a softener with a carbon stage. The plumber can lay out which pieces go where. Other fixture and pipe work is listed on the services page.

Keeping a Softener Working Year After Year

A softener needs very little attention, but a few habits keep it effective.

  • ✓
    Check the salt. Look in the brine tank every month or two and top it up before it runs low.
  • ✓
    Break up salt bridges. A hard crust can form above the water line and stop the brine from forming. Tap it loose with a broom handle.
  • ✓
    Test the hardness. A simple test strip on a softened tap tells you if the unit is still doing its job.
  • ✓
    Schedule a service visit. Resin beds wear out over many years. A visit now and then catches problems early.

If hard water has already left scale in the tank, a flush at the same visit helps clear it. The water heater repair page describes that work.

Local note

Where Suisun City's Water Comes From

The Suisun-Solano Water Authority supplies the city with surface water from Lake Berryessa, delivered by the Putah South Canal. It is treated at the Cement Hill Water Treatment Plant and disinfected with chlorine.

The City's 2025 water quality report records hardness of 170 ppm from a March 2025 sample. The 2020 report listed 160 ppm, so the level has stayed in the same hard band, and a softener sized for it will keep working.

Neighbors differ. Fairfield's 2022 report averaged 162 ppm with a range of 72 to 229 ppm, so a softener sized for a home in Fairfield may not match one here. In Vacaville, the city's wells ranged from 76 to 300 ppm, which means your street's water can be softer or harder than the town average.

Source: City of Suisun City 2025 Water Quality Report.

The best time to add a softener is when a water heater is being replaced, because the plumber already has the lines open. A softened supply protects a new tank or tankless unit from scale from the first day.
