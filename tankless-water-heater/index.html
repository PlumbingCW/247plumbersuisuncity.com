Tankless Water Heater Service in Suisun City, CA

Tankless units heat water as you use it, which saves space and ends the cold-tank problem. In Suisun City's hard water they also need a yearly flush, and that is where most owners get caught out.

Why Hard Water Matters More With a Tankless Unit

The City's 2025 water quality report lists hardness at 170 ppm, about 9.9 grains per gallon. On the common USGS scale that is in the hard band, near the top of it.

A tankless heater pushes water through a narrow heat exchanger at high temperature. Calcium and magnesium bake onto those narrow passages, shrink the flow and force the unit to work harder until it throws an error or stops heating.

That is the reason descaling is a service call, not a luxury. A yearly flush and a softener upstream keep the exchanger clear and protect the unit's life.

Signs Your Tankless Unit Needs Attention

These are the common warning signs that bring owners to the phone.

Error codes on the display

A code that keeps returning usually points to scale, a vent issue or a failed sensor. The code helps the plumber bring the right part.

Water that runs lukewarm

A heat exchanger coated in scale cannot transfer heat the way it should. The water stays warm but never gets hot.

Cold sandwich between hot bursts

A short blast of cold water between two hot bursts is common in tankless systems. Frequent or worsening gaps point to flow or sensor trouble.

Unit shuts off mid-shower

Overheating or a flow sensor fault can cut the burner. A plumber can test the sensors and clear any blockage.

How a Descaling Flush Works

The process is short, and it makes a visible difference in hot water output.

1

Isolate the unit

The plumber closes the gas and water supplies and opens the service valves on the unit.

2

Circulate a descaling solution

A pump pushes a mild acid solution through the heat exchanger, dissolving mineral buildup from the inside.

3

Rinse and clean the filter

Clean water flushes out the loosened scale. The inlet screen is cleaned before it is put back.

4

Restore and test

Service valves are closed, gas and water are restored and the unit is tested for temperature and flow.

Tankless or Tank: A Practical Comparison

Neither is right for every house. These are the trade-offs that tend to decide it.

Standard tank

  • Lower up-front cost
  • Simpler to install in most homes
  • Holds a reserve of hot water
  • Wastes some heat while standing

Tankless

  • Usually costs more up front but can cost less to run
  • Takes far less space on the wall
  • Delivers hot water without waiting for a tank to refill
  • Needs descaling and proper venting

For a quick look at tank models, see the water heater installation page.

What a Tankless Installation Requires

A tankless unit is not a simple swap for a tank. It draws a lot of fuel quickly, so the supporting systems often need to change.

Gas supply

A gas tankless heater needs enough gas flow to fire at full output. An older line sized for a tank may need to be upgraded.

Venting

These units use specific vent materials and routes. The plumber checks where the vent can exit and whether clearances are met.

Power and water

Most units need an electrical outlet for controls, plus isolation valves for service. A condensate drain may also be needed on high-efficiency models.

Local note

California Bracing Law Applies Here Too

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement, following the California Plumbing Code. It covers standard units of 120 gallons or less.

For a wall-hung tankless unit, the mounting and anchoring are part of how the plumber meets that rule. Ask how your unit will be secured, and ask the Suisun City Building Division whether the job needs a permit. The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), is what the plumber follows for the vent material and route and for the gas connection, which matters more on a tankless unit than on a tank.

Source: California Health and Safety Code section 19211.

Tankless Safety Checklist: Gas, Exhaust and Hot Water

A tankless unit burns gas and runs hot, so a few safety habits are worth knowing before anything goes wrong.

  • ✓
    Gas smell. Do not touch switches or use a phone inside. Leave the house, call 911 and then call us from outside.
  • ✓
    Venting and carbon monoxide. Keep the vent clear and sealed. A CO alarm near the bedrooms is a sensible backup, and sooty marks or a blocked vent mean the unit stays off until it is checked.
  • ✓
    Scald risk. Tankless units can deliver very hot water. Keep the temperature setting moderate, especially with children or older adults in the house.
  • ✓
    What to shut off. Close the gas valve at the unit and the cold water isolation valve, and switch off its power. These are the three shutoffs to know before an emergency.

If the unit shows a repeating error code or leaks at a fitting, shut it off as above and call the 24/7 line.

Repair, Descale or Replace the Unit

Many tankless problems are fixable. Sensor faults, dirty inlet screens and scale are all common, and the plumber can usually correct them in one visit.

A heat exchanger that has been scaled badly for years may be past saving. In that case, replacing the unit and adding water treatment is the better path than another descaling.

Because these units are costly, it makes sense to look at the maintenance habit as well as the fix. A yearly flush costs far less than the damage it prevents.
