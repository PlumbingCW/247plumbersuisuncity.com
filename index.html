Emergency guidance

Water Everywhere? Do This Before the Plumber Arrives

The first few minutes decide how much damage you end up with. These four moves are safe for any homeowner and cost nothing.

1

Find the main shutoff

Turn the main valve clockwise until it stops. It is usually near the water meter, at the front of the house, or where the supply line enters the garage or utility room.

2

Kill power near water

If water is pooling around outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, leave that area alone and switch off power only if the breaker box is dry and you can reach it safely.

3

Cut gas to a failed heater

For a gas water heater that is leaking or smells odd, turn the gas control to off. If you smell gas at all, get everyone outside and call 911 first.

4

Move what you can

Lift rugs, electronics and boxes off wet floors, and open a bucket or towel dam under the drip. Take a few photos for your records before cleanup starts.

Once the water is off, you have time to describe the problem calmly. For a split tank, read water heater replacement. For a pipe spraying inside a wall or ceiling, see burst pipe repair.

Hot Water Problems: Repair, Replace or Install

A tank that leaks from the base is finished, because the steel has corroded through and no part will fix that. In that case water heater replacement is the answer, and we work out the right size for your household. If the water is just lukewarm or the unit rumbles, water heater repair is often enough.

Planning a remodel or moving the heater to a new spot? Water heater installation covers venting, gas and electrical connections. California law requires water heaters to be braced or strapped against earthquake motion, and a proper install includes that.

A tankless water heater costs more up front than a tank unit but can cost less to run. Hard water leaves scale inside the heat exchanger, so tankless owners in town need a regular flush. Plumbing work follows the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). For you, that means a set standard, and the Building Division can say if a permit applies.

What Hard Water Does to Your Plumbing

The City's 2025 water quality report lists Suisun City tap water at 170 ppm hardness. That is about 9.9 grains per gallon, and on the common USGS scale it sits in the hard band, close to the top.

Hard water leaves chalky scale on faucet aerators, showerheads and the bottom of tank heaters. Scale makes a heater work harder and can shorten its life, so flushing the tank and checking the anode rod are worth doing. A water softener or filtration system is the long-term fix if the buildup bothers you.

Drains, Sewer Lines and Backups

One slow sink usually means a local clog, and drain cleaning with a cable clears it. When a line clogs again a few weeks later, grease or scale is coating the pipe walls, and hydro jetting scours them clean with high-pressure water.

If the tub gurgles while the washer drains, or the lowest fixture in the house floods, the problem is probably in the main line. That is a job for sewer line cleaning, and a camera run through sewer camera inspection shows what caused it.

When the camera finds a crack, an offset joint or roots, sewer line repair covers the options from a spot fix to lining or replacement.

Local note

Who Owns the Sewer Lateral?

In Suisun City, the City runs about 70 miles of smaller gravity sewer mains, and the Fairfield-Suisun Sewer District owns the larger trunk mains and the treatment plant. The pipe from your building to the main is a private lateral, and the property owner is responsible for it. The District says it does not repair private laterals.

That split matters when a backup hits. If sewage is coming up in your home, the City asks residents to call Police Dispatch right away so the correct crew is sent, and then call a plumber to clear and inspect your own line.

Source: City of Suisun City sewer and wastewater information and the Fairfield-Suisun Sewer District FAQ.

Leaks, Pipes and Soft Marsh Ground

Suisun City sits next to Suisun Marsh, and local history records that the town was built on marshy land. Ground that shifts and settles can stress buried pipe over time. A hidden leak under a yard or floor often shows up first as a high water bill or a meter that keeps spinning, and leak detection finds it without tearing up the whole house.

A warm spot on the floor or the sound of running water when everything is off points to slab leak repair. A wet, soggy patch in the yard between the meter and the house points to water line repair.

Much of the housing here dates from the late 1970s through the 1990s, so older supply lines can develop pinhole leaks and discolored water. If that sounds familiar, repiping replaces the aging pipe in one planned job.

Fixtures, Remodels and Commercial Calls

Small fixture problems turn into big ones when ignored. A running or leaking toilet wastes water around the clock, so toilet repair is worth a quick call. A dripping spout or a shower valve that will not close is a job for faucet repair, and a humming or jammed sink unit needs garbage disposal repair.

New sinks, tubs, showers and dishwasher hookups fall under plumbing installation. For restaurants, shops and offices, commercial plumbing covers after-hours repairs, grease traps and backflow testing.
