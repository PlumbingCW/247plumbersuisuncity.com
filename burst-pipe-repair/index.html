Stop the Water: The First Ten Minutes

Where you shut off the water matters more than anything else you do before a plumber arrives.

1

Find the main shutoff

It is usually a valve near the front of the house, by the water heater or in a garage wall. A quarter-turn lever or a round wheel are the two common types.

2

Close it all the way

Turn a wheel clockwise or swing a lever across the pipe. Open a low faucet afterward to drain what is left in the system.

3

Kill power near the water

If water is dripping near outlets, light fixtures or a panel, turn off those circuits at the breaker only if you can reach it while dry.

4

Contain and call

Put buckets, towels and a wet vac to work. Then call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher where the water is coming from.

Pipe Burst? Do This Before We Arrive

These steps limit damage to floors and belongings in the time before a plumber reaches you.

1

Empty the lines

With the main closed, open the lowest tap in the house and flush toilets once. Less water in the pipe means less to spill.

2

Shut off the water heater

Switch off power to an electric unit or set gas to pilot. A tank feeding a broken line can keep pushing hot water out.

3

Move the valuables

Pull electronics, paper and rugs from the wet area first. Furniture legs can be propped on foil or plastic to protect finishes.

4

Document the damage

Take photos and short videos of the break and wet surfaces before cleanup. Your insurer will likely ask for them.

5

Know when to leave

If water is pouring through a light fixture or the ceiling is sagging heavily, stay out of that room and tell the dispatcher.

Why Pipes Burst in a Mild Climate Like Suisun City

Freezing is uncommon in a warm-summer Mediterranean climate, so most bursts here trace to something else.

Corroded or worn pipe

Old galvanized, thin copper or tired fittings can fail without warning, usually at a joint or an already weak patch.

High water pressure

Pressure that runs above normal stresses every fitting in the house. A failing pressure regulator can set up a burst in a weak spot.

Scale and hard water

At 170 ppm, local tap water leaves mineral buildup that can narrow pipes and crust joints, which adds to wear over time.

Ground and structure movement

Buried lines in soft marsh soil can be stressed by shifting ground. Sags in a crawlspace or under a slab can pull on a pipe until it splits.

A Temporary Patch Versus a Real Repair

Clamps and tape can buy time. They do not turn a broken pipe into a sound one.

Temporary measures

  • Rubber and hose clamp wraps
  • Epoxy putty on a pinhole
  • Self-fusing tape
  • Pipe caps on a dead-end stub
  • Shutting a branch valve and leaving it

A proper repair

  • Cut out the damaged section
  • Join new pipe with a fitting made for that pipe material
  • Test pressure before closing the wall
  • Replace nearby pipe if it looks worn
  • Explain the price before work starts

A patch is fine as a bridge until a plumber arrives. Leaving one in place for months invites a second failure at the same spot.

Drying Out After the Water Is Gone

Stopping the spray and fixing the pipe is only half the job. Wet drywall, insulation and carpet padding keep holding moisture, and mold can start within a couple of days.

Open the wall or ceiling cavity as needed, run fans and dehumidifiers and let the structure dry before closing it up. The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), also expects the repaired line to be pressure tested and to hold before the wall is sealed. Call your insurer early, and ask what documentation they need.

When a Burst Points to a Bigger Problem

A single break in a recently repaired line is bad luck. A second or third break in the same house suggests the whole system is wearing out.

Patterns worth taking seriously

Rust-colored water, low pressure, mineral crust on joints and several leaks in a short time all point to aging supply pipe. Much of the housing here dates from the late 1970s through the 1990s, so older supply lines are worth checking.

What to do about it

A whole-house repiping plan replaces the supply system in one project. If the breaks are hard to place, leak detection can map which sections are failing.
