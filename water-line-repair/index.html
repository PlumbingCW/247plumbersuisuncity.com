Where the Utility Ends and Your Line Begins

The water line is the buried pipe that carries water from the meter to your house. Before any digging, find out where the utility's side stops and your side starts, since that decides who handles each stretch.

In Suisun City, water comes through the Suisun-Solano Water Authority, and the City Finance Department handles service questions. Ask them where the meter connection ends, and a plumber can then work on the private run to the building.

Symptoms That Point to the Line Between Meter and House

Service line problems usually show up outdoors first, but not always.

A wet or soft patch in the yard

Grass that stays green in dry weeks, a spongy spot or a puddle with no rain nearby often marks a leak over the line.

Low pressure at every tap

If the whole house loses pressure at once, the supply may be restricted or leaking before it reaches your plumbing.

The meter turns with the house valve closed

Close the main valve inside or at the house. If the dial keeps moving, water is escaping in the buried line between the meter and the valve.

Dirty or discolored water

Brown or cloudy water after a pressure change may mean a cracked or corroded line is drawing in soil or rust.

Line leaking

Water Line Break? Do This Before We Arrive

A broken service line can move a lot of water quickly. These steps keep it contained.

1

Close the house valve

Turn the main at the house clockwise. This stops flow into the building but may not stop water leaking outside before the valve.

2

Check the meter shutoff

Many meters have a valve on the street side. Use a meter key or pliers only if you can reach it safely, and tell the dispatcher what you did.

3

Keep people off the area

A soaked yard can hide a washout. Keep children and pets away from the wet spot and any soft edges.

4

Note the signs

Photograph the wet area and the meter dial. Mark the spot with a flag or stone so the technician can find it fast.

Do not dig the line yourself. Striking a gas, electric or other buried line is far worse than a leaking water pipe.

Why Buried Lines Fail in Soft Marsh Ground

Suisun City was built on marshy land, and local history records the railroad roadbed across the marsh settling 6 to 12 inches a year until fill and drainage steadied it. Buried pipe in soil that moves, even a little, can pull apart at joints or crack where the ground settles unevenly.

Tide and storm flooding are a documented local concern, so wet soil around a buried line is also normal here. That makes it harder to tell a pipe leak from a high water table by sight alone, which is why a meter test and a leak detection visit help before anyone digs.

Trenchless Replacement Versus Open Trench

There are two main ways to replace a failed service line. The choice depends on what sits above the pipe.

Open trench

  • A trench is dug along the full pipe route
  • Old pipe is removed and new pipe laid in place
  • Best when the line is shallow and the yard is open
  • Easy to inspect joints and bedding
  • Leaves landscaping, walks or paving to restore

Trenchless methods

  • Small access pits are dug at each end
  • New pipe is pulled or pushed along the old route
  • Best under driveways, patios and mature plantings
  • Less surface disruption
  • Needs a line in good enough shape to be followed

A technician looks at the route, the pipe condition and the surface above it before suggesting a method. The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), governs the pipe material, depth and shutoff valve on a new service line, so the plan has to fit it.

Pressure, Hardness and the Life of Your Supply Line

Hard water does not cause every service line failure, but it contributes. Tap water here measured 170 ppm hardness in the City's 2025 report, which is in the hard band on the common USGS scale. Scale builds inside older metal pipe and narrows it, which cuts flow and speeds up wear.

If your water line is metal and you notice weaker pressure over time, scale and corrosion may be part of the story. A softener or filtration system addresses buildup downstream of the meter. It does not replace a failing buried line.

If the Trouble Is Inside the House Instead

Sometimes the meter test shows the leak is on the house side. A spray or sudden break in a wall is a burst pipe repair call, and a leak under the floor falls under slab leak repair.
